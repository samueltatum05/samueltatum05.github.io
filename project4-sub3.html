Deep Space Probe Power System

Summary

This project addressed the power generation challenge facing deep-space exploration: how to reliably convert nuclear heat into usable electricity when solar power is no longer viable. To generate 50 kW of thermal input into meaningful electrical output for a deep-space probe, we analyzed the thermodynamic trade-offs between component efficiency, working fluid selection, and system-wide entropy generation. By prioritizing minimal irreversibility across the entire Brayton cycle, we engineered a closed-loop power system integrating a helium-based two-stage compression train with intercooling, a high-effectiveness recuperator, and a radiative heat-rejection panel sized for the vacuum of space. This architecture squeezes usable shaft work from a compact, radioisotope-driven heat source while rejecting waste heat passively, with no moving parts, through engineered heat pipes and space-rated radiator coatings. Ultimately, the design demonstrates a scalable framework for autonomous, long-duration power generation critical for enabling the next generation of deep-space missions where no other energy source is available.
