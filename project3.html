Competition Robotics

The Big Picture

Led mechanical engineering design, rapid iterative prototyping, and drive execution for FTC Team 7244 ("Out of the Box"), maintaining a consistent ranking within the top 10–20 teams globally. Directed the CAD lifecycle and custom fabrication of six World Championship-level robots through integrating PID-controlled drivetrains with functional odometry for precise autonomous navigation and using trade-off decision matrices/engineering design reviews with industry mentors. Engineered novel subsystem designs, including CNC-machined frames and carbon-fiber mechanisms, that set performance benchmarks still widely adopted by elite robotics teams across the nation.

Relic Recovery (2017–2018) — 1st Year on Team

The Game

The central engineering constraint was static balance: robots had to begin and end matches fully supported on a raised, unstable "Balancing Stone" platform, with no contact allowed between the platform's outer edge and the floor — meaning any drivetrain with poor weight distribution or sloppy end-of-match positioning risked a fall and lost points. Mid-match, teams needed an intake and stacking subsystem capable of manipulating foam cube "Glyphs" into a vertical, rail-constrained "Cryptobox," where scoring depended on pattern-matching a randomized cipher across rows and columns. Finally, an end-game manipulator had to transport an irregularly shaped "Relic" off the field entirely and deposit it in one of three tiered zones, ideally upright for a bonus.

Our Solution

We designed a custom CNC-machined drivetrain in CAD, chain-driven with a 6-wheel drop-center configuration: grippy, thick-rubber Colson wheels in the center for traction and precise balance on the Balancing Stone, sandwiched by omni wheels on either end for clean turning performance. The intake was a dual-staged, all-omni-wheel roller system with an opening/closing jaw — one Glyph would be intaken and driven upward into a second stage while a second Glyph was picked up simultaneously, letting us hold two at once. The entire intake rode on a ball-bearing linear slide for vertical positioning, stacking Glyphs up to four high, with a servo-and-cam-driven kicker that extended a plastic bar to push each box into place in the Cryptobox. For the Relic, we 3D-printed a holder custom-molded to the object's contour, secured by a 3D-printed finger on a servo. That mechanism sat on its own linear slide with a wrist servo at the end to flex the Relic up to parallel-with-the-floor, clear the field wall, extend roughly 10 feet via a giant spool-and-string-driven linear slide, and retract.

Rover Ruckus (2018–2019) — 2nd Year on Team

The Game

This season introduced a vertical latching problem: robots started suspended from a "Lander" and had to lower themselves to the field, then in end game re-latch and hoist the entire robot back off the ground for the match's single highest point value. Autonomous added a computer-vision constraint — identifying and displacing the one gold mineral among a randomized field of gold and silver elements without disturbing the others. Driver-controlled play required a sorting intake that could differentiate mineral types on the fly and route them into correspondingly labeled cargo holds, since misplacement scored zero.

Our Solution

We cut and sanded insulation polypropylene foam and overlaid it with carbon-fiber cloth and epoxy to build a strong but lightweight robot base. The drivetrain was a simple four-wheel setup: rear-wheel direct drive on grippy Colson wheels, with the front two wheels as omnis acting essentially as casters. A central carbon-fiber arm, powered by a custom 3-motor 3:1 gearbox, doubled as both our hanging and scoring mechanism — a servo-actuated hook embedded in the arm would latch onto the Lander's handle and pull the robot off the ground to hang for up to 5 minutes at a time. That same arm rotated up to outtake and down to intake. The intake itself was two rows of roller wheels with some give, sweeping minerals up into a plastic hopper mounted at the end of the arm; a servo-driven lid held the game elements in place until the arm rotated upward to dump them into the scoring hold.

Skystone (2019–2020) — 3rd Year on Team

The Game

The defining challenge was manipulating a mobile scoring structure rather than a fixed one: teams had to grip and reposition a heavy, alliance-neutral "Foundation" into their building zone, then build directly on top of it while it could shift under load. Layered on this was a vision-based discrimination task in autonomous — locating and preferentially delivering visually marked "Skystones" from a shared loading zone before generic stones — all while driving the entire robot underneath a fixed-height "Skybridge" gate. Stacking multiple blocks without toppling the tower, then finishing with a precision-placed capstone, added a rigid-body stacking tolerance problem on top of the transport and vision demands.

Our Solution

We built a 4-wheel mecanum drivetrain on a custom CNC-machined aluminum frame, with functional PID-controlled odometry pods for driver assist. The intake used two wheels mounted parallel to the ground, counter-spinning to intake or outtake a stone, then hinging on a servo to hold it securely. Stones were lifted with a dual, continuously-strung six-stage linear slide driven by two DC motors, then extended outward in a praying-mantis-style motion via a virtual four-bar mechanism at the top of the slides, allowing precise placement onto the Foundation. We also added two servo-actuated hooks on the back of the robot to reposition the Foundation during end game.

Ultimate Goal (2020–2021) — 4th Year on Team

The Game

This game shifted the core problem from manipulation to ballistics: teams needed a launch mechanism capable of consistently propelling foam rings into goals at three distinct heights, plus three narrow "Power Shot" targets, feeding from an intake and indexing system that could pick rings off the floor and queue them for rapid-fire cycles. In parallel, robots needed a separate gripping subsystem to transport a "Wobble Goal" to a randomized target zone determined in real time by reading a randomized field configuration.

Freight Frenzy (2021–2022) — 5th Year on Team

The Game

The engineering emphasis this season was cycle-time optimization under mechanical variety: robots had to collect three different freight geometries from a shared warehouse and deliver them to one of three height-tiered levels on a shipping hub that itself had to be left balanced at match end. Autonomous required a barcode/vision-recognition step to identify a randomized scoring level for a preloaded box, directly tying perception accuracy to points.

Power Play (2022–2023) — 6th Year on Team

The Game

The dominant constraint became high-precision vertical placement under time pressure: cones had to be threaded onto spring-mounted poles at four different heights, up to 33 inches, rewarding fast, repeatable cycle times over any single high-value action. On top of raw placement, teams had to solve a graph-connectivity problem in real time — strategically claiming junctions to complete a "circuit" spanning alliance terminals — while a final precision-capping task in end game added a last, low-tolerance manipulation step.
