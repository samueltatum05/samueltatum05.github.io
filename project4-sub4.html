Thermal Energy Storage

Summary

This project addressed the fundamental intermittency problem limiting solar power adoption worldwide: energy is only collected when the sun shines, but demand exists around the clock. To bridge this gap, we analyzed the two-phase heat transfer dynamics linking a parabolic trough solar collector to a packed-bed thermal storage system, tracking how a phase-changing refrigerant carries concentrated solar energy into a bank of solid storage pellets. By modeling the coupled boiling, condensation, and transient conduction physics governing the system, we engineered a closed-loop storage architecture that captures six hours of concentrated solar flux and banks nearly 2,400 kWh of usable heat, releasing it as high-temperature air over an extended discharge period long after the sun has set. This approach eliminates the reliability gap that keeps solar thermal energy from displacing fossil-fired heating, using only phase-change physics and a solid storage medium with no batteries or moving parts required. Ultimately, the design demonstrates a scalable thermal energy storage framework essential for making solar power a dependable, round-the-clock resource in the global transition to renewable energy.
